# Obesity_level_estimation
This project explores a dataset of 2,111 individuals with 17 attributes related to demographics, lifestyle, and health behaviors to understand factors influencing obesity. Using Python (Pandas, Seaborn, Matplotlib), the analysis identifies key patterns and associations across age, gender, BMI, diet, activity, and habits
🔧 Project Steps

1.Data Loading & Overview

Imported dataset with Pandas, checked structure using head(), info(), and describe().

2.Data Pre‑processing

Handled missing values, duplicates, corrected data types, and encoded categorical variables.

3.Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Performed univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis to explore relationships between attributes.

4.Visualizations

Created plots (countplots, boxplots, violin plots) to highlight trends and associations.

5.Insight Generation

Summarized findings into clear, actionable points linking lifestyle factors with obesity risk.


🔑 Major Insights
Age & Gender: Most participants are ~20 years old; dataset is gender‑balanced, though obesity patterns differ by category.

BMI: Average BMI for obese individuals is above 30.

Family History: Strongly increases obesity risk, especially in Obesity Type I.

Diet: High‑calorie food consumption strongly linked to obesity; vegetarian diets do not guarantee protection.

Snacking: Frequency less important than snack quality.

Water Intake: Higher intake aligns with healthier weight categories.

Activity & Screen Time: More activity → less obesity; more screen time → higher obesity risk.

Smoking & Alcohol: Non‑smokers dominate; alcohol use mostly occasional, heavy drinking rare.

Transportation: Automobile use dominates, reinforcing sedentary patterns.

🎯 Key Takeaway
Obesity outcomes are shaped primarily by lifestyle behaviors — diet quality, hydration, physical activity, screen time, and transport choices — rather than demographics alone. Preventive strategies should focus on promoting active lifestyles, adequate hydration, reduced high‑calorie food intake, and awareness of sedentary risks.
